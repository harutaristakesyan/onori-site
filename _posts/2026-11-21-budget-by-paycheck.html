Most budgets are monthly because calendars are. But money does not arrive on the first of the month. It arrives on payday, and for a lot of people that is every two weeks, or twice a month, or on a date that drifts. A monthly budget asks you to hold two clocks in your head at once.

Where the monthly budget breaks

  • Rent meets the wrong paycheck. A monthly plan says rent is covered. It does not say whether this paycheck has to cover it, or the next one.
  • “Left this month” is not “left until I'm paid”. Having $600 left on the 20th means something very different if payday is tomorrow or in eleven days.
  • Three-paycheck months. Paid every two weeks, you get them twice a year, and a monthly budget has no place to put them.

Plan the paycheck instead

The alternative is to give every paycheck its own plan, running from that payday to the next. The bills due in that stretch are set aside first. What remains goes to a few envelopes and to savings. Nothing is assumed about money that has not arrived yet.

Once the plan follows the paycheck, one question has a real answer: how much can I spend today? Take what is left after the bills, divide it over the days until payday, and you have a number that is true this morning and adjusts itself tonight.
