One-liner

Onori is a paycheck budget for iPhone and Mac that shows one number, what you can spend a day until payday, and keeps the ledger in your own iCloud.

The story

Onori is built by a husband-and-wife team based in Armenia. Harut Aristakesyan writes the code, and the app runs the family's own budget every day. That's not a detail: Onori exists because they wanted a budget with two properties that rarely coexist, an answer you can use today and real privacy, and couldn't find one. It's also why sharing a household ledger between two phones is at the centre of the app rather than a checkbox on a feature list: it's the way the people who make it use it.

Instead of connecting to banks through aggregators or bundling yet another paid chatbot, Onori made a bet: your iPhone and your iCloud are enough to run your budget, and the assistant you already use is enough to ask about it.
