Less to type. Nothing leaves your iPhone.

A budget only works if the purchases get into it. Onori keeps adding one by hand to a few taps, and with Plus it does most of the adding itself: no bank logins, no aggregators, no server reading your transactions.

Three ways a purchase gets in

1 · By hand, in a few taps

Amount, merchant, done. Onori remembers the category a merchant had last time. Siri, Shortcuts, a widget and a Control Center button add one without opening the app. This part is free.

2 · Apple Pay, on its own

A Shortcuts automation hands each Apple Pay purchase to Onori. With Plus, the purchase saves itself and is counted at once; the first one is on the house, so you can see how it works.

3 · A receipt, scanned

Scan a receipt and the merchant, total, date and category are read on your iPhone with Apple Intelligence. Every receipt still goes through review before it counts.

Why this beats bank connections

  • Works with any bank and any card. There is nothing to connect, so there is no list of supported banks.
  • No credentials shared. Bank-linking apps ask for your online-banking login through a third party. Onori never asks, because it never connects.
  • Counted once. If a receipt belongs to a purchase that is already recorded, linking attaches it to that purchase instead of adding a second one.
  • You stay in control. A purchase Onori was not sure about waits for you in review. The receipt's photo is kept with the transaction and syncs through your own iCloud.

Bills that mark themselves

A recurring bill can mark itself as paid, so the bills list stays true without a tap. That is part of Plus too. By hand, one tap on Pay does the same, even when you pay early.
